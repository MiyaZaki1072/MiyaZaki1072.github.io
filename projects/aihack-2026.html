1. SUMMARY

Models built for Aihack Thailand 2026, an AI hackathon on finance data run by AIFUL, a Japanese consumer lender, with Chulalongkorn Business School. The selection round asked teams to predict the probability that a loan applicant defaults within 12 months, using only what is known at application time. Scoring was ROC-AUC on a hidden private test set, and the top 30 teams advanced to the on-site final.

2. RESULT

14th of 103 teams (476 participants) on the private leaderboard, with ROC-AUC 0.6628 (0.66275); first place scored 0.6648. Qualified for the final, held on site at Chulalongkorn Business School on December 8–10, 2026.

3. FULL NOTES

Where we started

I was new to machine learning when the competition started. We began with gradient-boosted trees (LightGBM, CatBoost and XGBoost) on engineered features, then moved to AutoGluon. Two days before the deadline we were 46th on the leaderboard.

What changed

Instead of tuning more, we tested new model types one at a time and kept only what clearly improved the score. The breakthrough was pretrained tabular foundation models on the raw columns. TabPFN-3.5 became our strongest single model, and adding NVIDIA Kumo Tabular and then LimiX-2 each lifted the score further. The final submission was a rank-averaged ensemble of the three plus a small denoising-autoencoder neural network.

I led the model experiments and built the final ensemble. My teammate Kongpop Ariyapattanaporn built the LimiX-2 model.

Score progression

Private-leaderboard ROC-AUC, revealed after the deadline:

  • LightGBM baseline: 0.6546
  • Best gradient-boosting / neural-net blend: 0.6558
  • AutoGluon ensemble: 0.6578
  • TabPFN-3.5 alone: 0.6611
  • + NVIDIA Kumo Tabular: 0.6620
  • + LimiX-2 (final ensemble): 0.6628

What I learned

  • Change the model, not the weights. Changing the model family moved the score far more than tuning ensemble weights: about 30 weight variants of the same models all landed within 0.001 AUC.
  • Trust honest cross-validation. Small public-leaderboard differences are noise.
  • Data privacy is a hard rule. No competition data was ever shared with AI tools, models ran in private sessions, and all data was deleted after the round as required.
